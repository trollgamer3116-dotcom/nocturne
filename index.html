About the model

The ocean and garden are a visual stage. Their arrangement is schematic; the clock dial also moves the camera through a schematic viewing range, not to the physical clock radius.

Supported stationary clocks use Schwarzschild time dilation outside a nonrotating, uncharged black hole. The garden clock is at 50 horizon radii. The sea follows your clock; growth and rain follow the garden’s clock as an accelerated visual metaphor. These are not falling clocks or biological predictions.

The ocean is masked out wherever the black-hole image appears. This keeps the exhibit visually separate from its foreground stage; it is not a physical ocean near a black hole. The lensed sky uses sampled distant-observer null geodesics and a tilted thin disk. Finite camera placement, sampling and finite path length approximate the image. Disk palette, turbulent filaments, close emission layers, temporal interpolation and ring highlights are artistic; the paths still use the Schwarzschild solver.

Water combines finite-depth gravity waves with a two-dimensional damped height-field wave equation for touch disturbances. Fixed time steps, absorbing boundaries and bounded input keep it stable. It models surface waves, not a full Navier–Stokes fluid. Reflected geometry comes from a mirrored camera. Underwater caustics, tiny foam accents and glass refraction are visual approximations.

Clock beacons are stationary sources already transmitting. Pulse rates follow gravitational frequency shifts; their visible arrangement is schematic.
